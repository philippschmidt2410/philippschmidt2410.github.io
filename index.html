PhD Student in Economics, LMU Munich

Philipp Schmidt

I study how trade and technology shocks travel through labour markets, using shift-share designs and worker-level administrative data.

I am a PhD student at the Munich Graduate School of Economics, LMU Munich, supervised by Monika Schnitzer and Carsten Eckel. Before starting my PhD, I completed a double master’s degree at the University of Mannheim and the Toulouse School of Economics and worked as a research assistant at ZEW, GESIS and the University of Mannheim.

Research

Work in Progress

One Shock, Two Europes: The (Asymmetric) Labor Market Effects of Chinese Import Competition

I estimate the effect of Chinese import competition on manufacturing employment across EU-15 regions, using a shift-share instrument in the Borusyak–Hull–Jaravel framework with exposure-robust inference. The aggregate effect is close to zero, but it conceals a sharp divergence between German regions and the rest of the EU-15.

Master’s thesis, Toulouse School of Economics, 2026. Supervisor: Paul Diegert, PhD.

Discussion Papers

Labor Market Integration of Asylum Seekers in Europe: Recent Trends and Barriers

Using the EU Labour Force Survey, we document how asylum seekers are integrating into labour markets across Europe and compare them with natives and economic migrants. Their employment rates are on average about 10 percentage points below those of natives, and the jobs they hold are of lower quality. Self-reported barriers to employment point to institutional hurdles as a main cause.

Auf Basis der Europäischen Arbeitskräfteerhebung beschreiben wir, wie sich Asylsuchende in Europa in den Arbeitsmarkt integrieren, im Vergleich zu Einheimischen und Arbeitsmigranten. Ihre Beschäftigungsquote liegt im Durchschnitt rund zehn Prozentpunkte unter der von Einheimischen, und ihre Stellen sind von geringerer Qualität. Die selbst berichteten Hürden bei der Jobsuche deuten vor allem auf institutionelle Ursachen hin.

Martin Lange, Sarah McNamara and Philipp Schmidt (2024). ZEW Discussion Paper No. 24-073.

Policy Reports

Migration and Integration in European Labour Markets

This report for the Horizon Europe project WeLaR examines how migrants fare in European labour markets, covering employment, income, job quality, skill mismatch and the use of welfare benefits. It draws on EU Labour Force Survey microdata for the EU, Norway, Iceland, Switzerland and the UK, and adds administrative evidence from Germany on the wage effects of the 2004 EU enlargement.

Der Bericht für das Horizon-Europe-Projekt WeLaR untersucht, wie sich Migrantinnen und Migranten auf europäischen Arbeitsmärkten behaupten, mit Blick auf Beschäftigung, Einkommen, Jobqualität, Qualifikationspassung und Sozialleistungsbezug. Grundlage sind Mikrodaten der Europäischen Arbeitskräfteerhebung für die EU, Norwegen, Island, die Schweiz und das Vereinigte Königreich, ergänzt um administrative Daten aus Deutschland zu den Lohneffekten der EU-Osterweiterung 2004.

Martin Lange, Sarah McNamara, Philipp Schmidt, César Barreto, Katrin Sommerfeld and Martin Streng (2024). Horizon Europe Deliverable D5.5, European Commission.
